Shortbot Post-Mortem

In five weeks the first bot sent three orders, all in one stock, all on its first day, and none of them shorts. After that it never traded again. Below is what happened and why, and what the new Stock Shorting Bot does differently.

Root causes, most damaging first

Why it failed

1

The trade gate could never open

The automated path only shorts names rated SHORT with HIGH conviction. During scoring, any name without a base-rate sample (exp_n) is stepped down one conviction level. But the base-rate data was attached after scoring, so every row was missing exp_n when it was scored. HIGH always became MEDIUM, the order list was empty every day, and nothing reported it as a failure.

Evidence: daily.py:74–75 calls score_all() and then expectancy.enrich(). scoring.py:258–263 does the step-down. daily.py:145 filters on conviction == "HIGH". Alpaca has no orders after Sep 15.

2

Failures were silent

Every run sent its output to a log file inside a git-ignored folder. A day with no trades looked exactly like a day where nothing ran, and nothing outside the laptop checked either one. Then the folder was renamed. When the project files were deleted, the journal and logs went with them.

Evidence: the crontab redirects to output/shortbot.log; .gitignore excludes output/; there is one cron error mail, from Oct 20.

3

The laptop was an unreliable host

launchd calendar triggers never fired on this Mac (runs = 0). cron needed Full Disk Access because the project sits on the Desktop. The Mac is set to sleep 1, and a closed lid puts it to sleep no matter what wake schedule is set. Any run during that window simply didn't happen.

Evidence: the old README's "Why cron and not launchd" and "Staying awake" sections; pmset -g still shows sleep 1 today.

4

Day-one order mechanics

Market orders went in at 15:59, inside the closing scramble, and the bot bought, sold and re-bought the same name in 54 seconds. That matches two failure modes the README describes later: a "run twice" flatten that sells what was just bought, and a late market order that misses the bell.

Evidence: the three CLNN orders above; the README sections "Why market-on-close" and "Running it twice".

5

The strategy's own backtest found no edge

Even with perfect execution, shorting the spikes lost money on average. Over 2,410 spike events across 600 small caps, close to close, shorts averaged −0.81% despite winning 52% of the time. 11.2% of shorts lost more than 10%, and the worst 1% lost about 40%. The README's own conclusion: "These weights rank fit; they do not forecast."

6

Data gaps and scope drift

There was no borrow-fee data, short interest was 1–3 weeks old, there was no news or catalyst check, and many small caps can't be shorted on Alpaca. The project also drifted: it started as a long-and-short "squeeze scanner", and its README says in one place that it doesn't place trades and in another that it trades automatically.

Changes in the new bot

What the Stock Shorting Bot does differently

FailureNew approach
Gate never openedNo conviction tiers. Every day it ranks 20 names and shorts the top 5 that pass the hard filters. A dry run prints the exact orders, and a run that ends with no orders is logged as an error.
Silent failuresEvery run, candidate, order and fill is written to Supabase. A heartbeat shows on the dashboard, and a missed day shows up as a red, stale card.
Lost historyTrades are rebuilt from Alpaca fills into the database, not into local CSV files.
Unreliable hostThe stale cron lines are replaced with absolute paths to the new folder, plus a setup guide for waking the Mac. The dashboard heartbeat exposes any missed run the next day.
Day-one order mechanicsOrders go in at 15:55, not 15:59. Positions held over are netted, never flatten-then-rebuy. A once-per-day guard blocks double runs, and order status is checked after submission.
No catalyst checkHeadlines from FMP or Yahoo are classified: real de-risking news is excluded, hype is rewarded. On its first dry run, using Oct 20 data, this removed the two names the score alone ranked #1 and #2: MYGN, up on a $210M asset sale to Labcorp, and BRZE, up 52% on a $5.66B Salesforce buyout.
Hard-to-borrow namesAlpaca's shortable and easy_to_borrow flags are a hard filter. The bot walks down the ranking until it has 5 names.

What this does not fix

The new rules are a different strategy from the one backtested, and they are untested. They hold 85% gross short exposure overnight with no stop. Spike shorts have fat-tailed losses: in the old backtest, one trade in nine lost more than 10%. It runs on paper only, and the dashboard will show within a few weeks whether it has an edge.
