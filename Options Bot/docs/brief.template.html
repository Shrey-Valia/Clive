Overnight research brief

Clive Overnight Brief

The dashboard is rebuilt as a working terminal. Options research covered 4 tracks, and 4 tuning agents tested about 500 strategy variants on real Alpaca option prices. All tuning was done on early data and judged only on data it never saw. In a strong bull market almost nothing beat simply holding the index. A few changes did hold up, and they are listed below. The two best fits for a $10k account are a dip-timed SPY put spread and a far out-of-the-money SPY iron condor held to expiry.

What to do with this

PAPER-TRADEDip put spread on SPY / QQQ / IWM

Sell a 7–14 day put spread after a sharp dip in an uptrend. Results: 93% win rate, out-of-sample Sharpe 1.01, and it works on a $10k account. It is in the market only about 27% of days.

PAPER-TRADEWheel QQQ with 0.15Δ calls

Sell lower-delta calls and roll tested calls for a credit. Results: 17.5% a year with a Sharpe of 1.11, beating the old default out of sample. It needs about $60k or more for one QQQ lot.

WATCHCovered call QQQ, hold to expiry

18.6% a year, Sharpe 1.22. The main gain comes from stopping the buyback of in-the-money calls at 21 DTE. It needs about $60k or more.

PAPER-TRADESPY iron condor, held to expiry

0.10Δ shorts, 3% wings, about 60 days, no stops. Out-of-sample Sharpe 1.46, max drawdown −6.8%, beta about 0.3. Fits $10k as one contract. A full loss is possible but hasn't happened in the sample yet.

DON'TEarnings plays, 1-day condors, entry filters

Every earnings structure and every 1DTE SPY/QQQ condor lost money after costs. VIX, 200-day and volatility-premium entry gates did not help out of sample.

The core fact

Options are usually overpriced, until they aren't

From 2016 to 2026, VIX sat above SPY's actual volatility over the next 21 days on 85% of days, by about 3.8 points on average. That spread is the volatility risk premium, and it is the only edge in premium selling with solid evidence behind it. The cost of collecting it shows up in a few crashes: on 2020-02-20 realized volatility came in 63 points above what VIX implied.

Twenty years of evidence

Filters don't time crashes. Emergency exits help a little.

Alpaca's option history only starts in 2024 and contains no bear market. So the regime filters were also tested on Cboe's put-writing index from 2007 to 2026, which covers 2008, 2020 and 2022. Each filter could change exposure only at the monthly roll, with a switching cost.

  • Entry gates did not help. No family of monthly entry gates (VIX/VIX3M, 200-day, volatility premium, vol targeting) beat plain put-writing OOS. A gate cannot get out before a fast crash, and it then sits out the rebound.
  • Intramonth kill switches helped. Exiting the day after VIX/VIX3M > 1.2, VIX > 40, or an 8% loss lifted OOS Sharpe from 0.50 to 0.64–0.79 and cut max drawdown from −29% to about −16%.
  • That benefit comes almost entirely from 2008 and 2020. Without those two crashes it is noise, and no filter passed White's reality check (p = 0.36). On the 2024–26 Alpaca data the kill switch hurt, because it sold near the V-shaped lows.
Credit spreads and iron condors

Hold far-out condors to expiry. Don't manage them.

This search covered 1,563 variants of put spreads and iron condors. One pattern held up clearly. Fewer, longer, far out-of-the-money condors held to expiry beat actively managed spreads, after costs. The baseline put spread loses money mostly because of its stops: they are checked only at the close, so gaps carry the loss past the stop (2.56x the credit on a 2x rule), and they sell at the lows.

  • F1, SPY iron condor: 0.10Δ shorts, 3% wings, about 60 DTE, held to expiry, max loss 20% of equity. Out-of-sample Sharpe 1.46, full-period CAGR 9.8%, max drawdown −6.8%, beta about 0.3. It works at $10k as one contract. All 15 trades won, and it gained +1.4% through the April 2025 crash.
  • F2, the same condor on SPY, QQQ and IWM: out-of-sample Sharpe 1.02. It works at $100k only.
  • Rejected: the RSI2 call-spread overlay, stops, laddering, trend/VIX filters and the kill switch. The overlay looked best in-sample, then lost 12.7% of equity on one trade in the 2026 rally.
  • The tail is untested. F1 never took a max loss in the sample. Its structural worst case is −20% of equity in one cycle, so size for that, not for the backtest drawdown. Its deflated Sharpe after 1,563 trials is about zero.

Limits to keep in mind

  • Daily closes only. Fills use the last trade, not quotes. Stops can gap far past their level, and greeks are inferred from prices.
  • Short sample. It covers 2.6 years in one mostly bull regime, with 30–130 trades per strategy. The universe was chosen with hindsight.
  • Costs are uncertain. The default model charges about 3x SPY's real bid-ask spread on narrow spreads. That's why plain put credit spreads looked like losers. With costs calibrated to real quote snapshots, the baseline SPY put spread goes from −2.8% to +2.8% a year. QQQ and IWM spreads really are wider, at 1.7–1.8x the default model.
  • Engine bugs found and worked around (each documented in a tuning/*/BUGS.md file):
    • Spread sizing on mismatched prints created one fictitious +$20k trade.
    • The kill switch's loss trigger can never fire on spreads.
    • The wheel trade log ignores share P&L.
    • The built-in covered call rolls in-the-money calls.
    • Signals fill on the same close that produced them; delay-by-one-day checks were run.
    None of the core engine files were changed, so these need a deliberate fix.
  • Paper only. Alpaca paper pays no interest on cash, which understates cash-secured strategies by about 2–5% a year.
