Part 1

Stock Shorting Bot

Design

How the shorting bot works

Each trading day the bot shorts five of the day's top gainers into the close and holds them until the next day's rotation. It runs from cron on the Mac against the Alpaca paper account.

Time (ET)StepDetail
15:40Startcron launches daily.py --execute. The bot reads the Alpaca calendar, so half days shift automatically to 12:45 / 12:55.
15:45ScanPulls TradingView's top gainers through its scanner API. Keeps US stocks with market cap above $25M and previous close above $3, top 20.
15:45EnrichShort interest and days to cover come from FINRA and yfinance. FINRA daily short volume, SEC fails-to-deliver, Alpaca shortable/easy-to-borrow flags, and headlines from Yahoo RSS (or FMP when a key is set).
15:45ScoreYour five-item checklist. Move and volume 35%, crowding 20%, FTD 10%, catalyst 35%. Borrow is a hard gate. Squeeze traps (DTC above 10 and SI above 20%) and real de-risking news are excluded.
15:55TradeCancels leftover orders, covers yesterday's book, then shorts the top five eligible names with market orders. Sizes run 19%, 18%, 17%, 16% and 15% of equity, about 85% gross. A name held over is resized rather than closed and reopened, which avoids wash-trade rejects.
16:15SyncEquity snapshot, positions, and round trips rebuilt from Alpaca fills since 2026-10-21. Everything goes to Supabase for the dashboard.

The catalyst check earned its keep on day zero

On the first test scan (Oct 20 data), the score alone ranked MYGN #1 and BRZE #2. MYGN was up on a $210M asset sale to Labcorp, and BRZE was up 52% on a $5.66B Salesforce buyout. Shorting a buyout target is the classic way to lose. The headline classifier excluded both names before any order was placed.

Post-mortem

What went wrong the first time

The first shorting bot ran from Sep 14 to Oct 20. In five weeks the account placed three orders, all CLNN, all on Sep 15 at 15:59, and none of them shorts. It lost $2.42. The full post-mortem is here.

  • The trade gate could never open. Scoring ran before base rates were attached, so every HIGH-conviction pick was downgraded to MEDIUM. The bot only traded HIGH.
  • Failures were silent. Logs went to a git-ignored folder, and nothing outside the laptop watched them.
  • The host was unreliable. launchd never fired, a closed lid put the Mac to sleep, and cron still pointed at a renamed folder.
  • Its own backtest found no edge. Spike shorts averaged −0.81% close-to-close at a 52% win rate. 11% of trades lost more than 10%.

The new bot fixes the first three. It has no conviction tiers, every run writes a heartbeat to Supabase, and the cron points at the right folder. The fourth point, whether the strategy has an edge at all, is what the historical backtest in section 4 is testing.

What day one revealed

  • Most of the gainer list can't be shorted on Alpaca. 11 of 20 names were blocked on Oct 21, and 12 of 20 on Oct 20. The bot falls through to weaker scores, which turned out to be 45 to 55 here, so the edge per pick is thinner than the checklist implies.
  • Today's moves were modest. Gains of +11% to +18% on low relative volume (0.7x to 1.8x) are well below the checklist's ideal blow-off (+20% to +100% on 5x to 10x volume).
  • Exposure is high. 85% gross short overnight with no stop. The post-mortem's backtest says single-name shorts like these have fat losing tails.

What the tuning taught, across families

  • Hold to expiry beats managing. Stops are checked at the close, so gaps overshoot them (2.56x credit on a 2x rule) and they sell at the lows.
  • Fewer round trips beats cheaper fills. The default cost model is about 3x real SPY spreads, but even with calibrated costs the managed put spread barely breaks even.
  • Lower-delta calls are the main lever for stock-backed strategies. In this up market, every short call is a drag.
  • Filters, kill switches and laddering hurt here. The sample's shocks were V-shaped, so de-risking sold near the lows.
  • In-sample rank barely predicts out-of-sample rank. The Spearman correlation is between −0.41 and 0.31 across families, so family-level patterns matter more than any single winner.

Live bot

Live options bot and code review

The paper-trading bot for the dip put spread is built in Options Bot/live/. It trades SPY, QQQ and IWM and enters at about 15:50 with a multi-leg limit order at the mid, re-priced until a 15:58 cutoff. Exits are at 50% of credit, a close above the 5-day average, after 5 days, or 1 day before expiry. Max loss is capped at 25% per position and 50% total, with a VIX kill switch. It uses only a separate Alpaca paper account's keys, defaults to dry run, and has 42 tests.

Today's dry run found no signal: SPY, QQQ and IWM all closed strong. It would have sized one SPY 761/746 put spread at $1.66 credit, $1,334 max loss.

Order-handling review: 16 findings to fix before going live

  • High:
    • A cron lock collision can silently skip a day's run, including an expiry exit.
    • A replace that races a fill can leave a spread untracked or overfilled.
    • A request timeout can leave two live orders.
    • An error mid-ladder leaves orders working.
    • The bot can't detect if the shorting bot's keys are pasted in, which would cancel the shorting bot's orders.
  • Medium:
    • A zero bid on the long leg blocks the exit near expiry.
    • Alpaca's 15:30 expiration-day handling isn't accounted for.
    • Assignment is not handled.
    • Duplicate order IDs can occur on half-dollar strikes.
  • Verified correct: leg construction, the credit/debit sign convention, cutoffs, the once-per-day guard and the paper-only refusals.

Caveats, bugs and next steps

Caveats

  • Short sample. The options data covers 2.6 years of mostly rising markets with three V-shaped shocks and no real bear market. Sharpe ratios carry about ±0.6 of error.
  • Daily bars only. Fills use last trades, not quotes. Greeks are inferred, and stops can't be tested intraday.
  • Hindsight in the universe. SPY, QQQ and the mega caps were chosen knowing they did well.
  • Interest not counted. Alpaca paper pays no interest on cash, which understates cash-heavy strategies by about 2–5% a year.
  • Shorting backtest limits. Historical shortability, borrow fees and market caps aren't available, so they are approximated. Delisted names are missing (survivorship bias).

Engine bugs found and worked around

These are documented in Options Bot/tuning/*/BUGS.md. The core files were not changed.

  • Spread sizing on mismatched prints booked one fictitious +$20k trade. A guard now skips spreads whose credit exceeds 60% of the width.
  • The kill-switch loss trigger can never fire on spreads.
  • The wheel's "above cost basis" rule silently falls back to strikes below basis, and its trade log omits share P&L.
  • The built-in covered call rolls ITM calls.
  • Signals fill on the same close that produced them. A one-day-lag check is reported for each finalist.
  • Forked workers crash on macOS. The fix is to set no_proxy=*.
