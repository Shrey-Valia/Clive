The live configuration loses money and can wipe out the account

The current rules hold 5 shorts overnight at 19% → 15% of equity each, about 85% gross, with no stop. Backtested from 2016 to 2026, they lost money in 10 of 11 years and in both halves of the data. Two single-day squeezes would have nearly ended the account: KOSS on 2021-01-26 (−98.7% of equity) and TOP on 2023-04-27 (−96.4%). The bot is running these rules on paper now. Nothing has been changed, pending your review.

The backtest rebuilt the 15:45 "top gainers" scan for every trading day using Alpaca minute and daily bars across 6,612 stocks, including 939 that are no longer listed. The rebuilt top 20 overlaps the daily-close version by 89.8%. The bot's own scoring code ran on historical data: FINRA short interest with a publication lag, SEC FTDs, and historical Alpaca/Benzinga headlines put through the same catalyst classifier. Costs scale by price and liquidity. Borrow is charged at 15% a year, or 50% for cheap or crowded names. Rules were chosen on 2016–2021 and tested on 2022–2026, with 1,037 trials counted.

The edge is real but sits in the wrong part of the hold

Stocks that spike into the close keep rising overnight, then fade during the next session. Holding from 15:55 to 15:55 collects both legs, so they cancel, and costs turn the result negative.

The proposed fix: short the next day intraday, smaller and pickier

Scan at 15:45 as today, but short the picks at the next day's open and cover at 15:55 that day. Hold up to 5 names at 10% each, 50% gross at most. Only names scoring 70+, priced at least $10, with at least $10M average daily volume.

Proposed changes, none applied yet

  1. Stop holding overnight. Short at the next open (09:31 cron) and cover at 15:55.
  2. Cut size to 10% per name, 50% gross at most.
  3. Add a minimum score of 70. Allow fewer than 5 names, or none.
  4. Add a minimum price of $10 and minimum average daily volume of $10M, on top of Alpaca's borrow check.
  5. Set the FTD weight to 0. Keep the catalyst and squeeze exclusions.

Caveats: there is no historical borrow data, so a price and volume gate stands in for it. Squeeze names were likely hard to borrow then, but don't rely on that. Borrow fees are assumed, and real fees on spiking names run 50–300% a year. Some delisted names are still missing. Small-cap fills at the open will be worse than modeled.
