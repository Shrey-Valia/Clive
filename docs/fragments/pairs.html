DON'T DEPLOYClassic pairs trading on US large caps

The papers find roughly zero after costs since about 2010. Our 2017–2026 backtest confirms it: every method was flat to slightly negative out of sample. It acts as a mild crash hedge and earns nothing the rest of the time.

CONTEXTWhere it used to work

Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst found about 1.44% a month from 1962 to 2002. Excess returns fell from 118 bp a month before 1989 to 38 bp after, and to about zero after 2010 as quant funds crowded in.

What the papers say

The reviewer read nine papers in full and summarized about 15 more. Notes are in Pairs Research/papers_pairs.md. Citations were found via web search and are not LibKey-resolved, and figures taken from abstracts are tagged [verify].

  • The classic method (Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst 2006). Form pairs over 12 months, trade them for the next 6, open at a 2σ gap and close when prices cross. Top-20 pairs earned 1.44% a month, or 0.90% when trading a day after the signal. Pairs stayed open about 3.75 months.
  • The edge decayed. After costs, 29 variants averaged +0.12% a month and were "largely unprofitable post 2002" (Do & Faff 2012). About 32% of pairs never converge.
  • Method comparison, 1962–2014, after costs (Rad, Low & Faff 2016). Distance earned 38 bp a month, cointegration 33 bp and copula 5 bp. Trading opportunities fell sharply after 2009.
  • Everything lost money in 2010–2015 (Clegg & Krauss 2018, S&P 500, 5 bp a side). Every method returned −0.2% to −2.8% a year after costs while the S&P made +13% a year. Machine-learning stat-arb lost 14–25% a year over the same span (Krauss, Do & Huck).
  • The strongest framework, ETF residuals (Avellaneda & Lee 2010). This trades each stock against its sector ETF. Sharpe was 1.1–1.44 over 1997–2007, falling to about 0.6–0.9 over 2003–07.
  • Crowding risk (Khandani & Lo). Generic stat-arb lost about 1–3% a day on Aug 7–9, 2007 as funds unwound together. It then rebounded +5.9% on Aug 10.
  • How pairs break. Pairs diverge for good, earnings or M&A news drives the gap, crowded unwinds trigger stops just before the snap-back, the short leg gets recalled or squeezed, and backtests suffer look-ahead and survivorship bias from today's index list.

Our backtest: 2017–2026 on Alpaca data

The universe was today's S&P 500, rebuilt point-in-time from yearly index snapshots to measure survivorship: 736 symbols, delisted names included. There were also 44 ETF pairs and 15 classic pairs such as KO/PEP, V/MA and XOM/CVX. Signals at the close traded at the next open. Costs ran 2–5 bp a side, and borrow was charged at 0.5% a year. Parameters were chosen on 2017–2021 and tested on 2022–2026. 61 runs were counted.

Why trades failed

  • Earnings. 72% of distance-method trades spanned an earnings date. Those averaged −1.2%, against +2.6% for trades that didn't. Skipping entries near earnings didn't fix it, because 50-day holds still hit the next report.
  • Real shocks. Globe Life fell 53% on a short-seller report (Apr 2024). Edison International fell 12% on the LA wildfires (Jan 2025). PLTR gapped 24% on earnings (Jan 2025).
  • Survivorship flattered the in-sample results. Using today's index list, cointegration showed an IS Sharpe of −0.23. The point-in-time list gives −1.03. Kalman's IS edge came largely from names like CVNA and PLTR before they joined the index.
  • Crash behavior. In Feb–Mar 2020, distance pairs gained +2.7% while SPY fell 33.5%. In 2022 they gained +1.5% against SPY's −18.2%. So it works as a small hedge, but it pays nothing the rest of the time.

Account size

Both account sizes are mechanically feasible on Alpaca. About 99% of the names it shorted are easy to borrow today, though that is today's flag, not a historical one. At $10k, whole shares leave legs 4–12% unbalanced, and 12–14% of signals can't be sized at all. At $100k the results match the fractional-share version, but returns stay at or below T-bill yields. Alpaca doesn't short fractional shares.
