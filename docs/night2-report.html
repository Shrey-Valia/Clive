Shorting bot v2

The 2016–2026 backtest showed the old rules lost money in 10 of 11 years and came close to wiping out the account on two squeezes (KOSS, TOP). Spike names drift up overnight and fade the next day, so v2 drops the overnight hold.

What the papers say about penny stocks

  • The overnight-up, intraday-down split is documented (Berkman et al.; Lou, Polk & Skouras: past overnight winners earn +3.47% a month overnight and −3.02% a month intraday). But about 45% of the overnight gain disappears when it's measured from the opening midpoint rather than the opening print.
  • Lottery-like and crowd-herded penny stocks underperform over the following weeks (Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw; Barber et al. on Robinhood; Kumar).
  • Costs dominate. About 65% of anomalies fail once microcaps are handled properly (Hou, Xue & Zhang). The average anomaly nets about 4 bp a month (Chen & Velikov).
  • Alpaca facts: no OTC stocks. Only 22–34% of recent penny gainers are easy to borrow. Overnight shorts under $5 need 100% margin.

Five rules from the papers: none beats the finalists

61 trials, with the best deflated Sharpe at 0.16.

  • Volatility-premium gate: noise. Its in-sample edge disappears with a one-day lag.
  • Expiry by VIX term structure: harmful.
  • Condor DTE × delta sweep: the in-sample pick (30 DTE) failed out of sample. 0.25Δ condors lost 47–53% at worst. The 60 DTE / 0.10Δ finalist stays.
  • News filters: no value. Closing a position on a news flag hurt every time.
  • Hype-fade call spreads: rejected. The "hype" label added nothing.
  • Worth watching: exiting the dip spread at the next open instead of 15:55 improved both periods (OOS Sharpe 2.08 vs 1.88). It wasn't the in-sample pick, so run it as a shadow paper rule first.

0DTE: no structure is positive after realistic costs

The test covered 290 configs on 1-minute SPY option bars across 234 sessions, including 22 FOMC, 32 CPI and 31 jobs-report days. Structures roughly break even before costs, and four legs of trading costs push them negative. The variance-premium filter reversed out of sample: +6.7% in-sample, −14.6% out of sample. FOMC days are "less bad" than ordinary days, not profitable. CPI-only flies (+3.4% out of sample, n = 15) are a hypothesis to paper-trade, not a result.

It also explains the earlier 1-day condor loss. On SPY alone the trade was +0.64% gross; QQQ and costs caused the −3.8%. All of SPY's gross edge (+2.06%, t 3.7) sits in the final 35 minutes before settlement, and Alpaca rejects orders on expiring SPY options after 15:30.

Part 4

What institutions know that the backtests miss

One agent checked every strategy against AQR, Man, Robeco, Cboe, Fed, BIS and SEC research. 33 of 35 journal articles were resolved through LibKey. The full review is in Research/institutional_review.md, with access links.

  • Both bots are the same bet. Shorting spikes, put spreads, condors, covered calls and pairs all sell insurance or supply liquidity, so they lose together in a volatility spike or a squeeze. Nothing currently stops a KOSS-type day from landing at the same time as a volatility gap.
  • Squeezes don't need high short interest. On Jan 27, 2021, KOSS rose 480% in one day, and 134 stocks had a one-day gain bigger than GameStop's largest. The short-interest filter is necessary but not sufficient.
  • VIX kill switches fire after the damage. Feb 2018, Aug 2024 and Apr 2025 were gap events with V-shaped recoveries. That's why the kill switch only helped in the slow crises of 2008 and 2020.
  • Covered calls are mostly long equity. About 10% of their risk is the volatility premium (Israelov & Nielsen). Collars often do worse than simply holding less stock.
  • The v2 design fits the evidence. Overnight-up, intraday-down is a documented effect (Lou, Polk & Skouras).

Their recommended risk framework

  • Shorting bot sizing: size by how much a squeeze would cost. 5% per name, a broker-side stop at +30–40%, at most 2 names per industry, no names with earnings within 2 days.
  • Options book: assume every spread can hit max loss at once. Cap total options max loss at 10% of the account and 3% per expiry. Treat SPY, QQQ and IWM as one position.
  • Account-level halts: −3% in a day means flatten and block. −10% drawdown means half size, and −15% means stop and review. Run a daily stress test across both bots.
  • Graduating from paper to live money:
    • Short bot: at least 60 trades and 9 months.
    • Options bot: at least 40 spreads, including a period with VIX above 25.
    • Slippage at most 1.5x the backtest's assumption.
    • 30 clean operating days in a row.
    • Then go live at 25% of target size, scaling up from there.

I applied the sizing ideas as optional switches and left your approved defaults in place. Whether to adopt them is your decision.

Read before acting

Caveats

  • Few trades, wide error bars. Every surviving strategy has a deflated Sharpe below 0.95 once all the trials behind it are counted. The shorting and flip results were chosen after more than 1,000 trials.
  • No historical borrow data. Backtests approximate it with price and volume filters, or with today's easy-to-borrow list. Real borrow on spike days was likely worse. The long sleeve now logs easy-to-borrow status daily so future tests can use real history.
  • Fills come from trade prints, not quotes. Opening-print fills on small caps will be worse than modeled, and the open-versus-midpoint gap is the main risk to the overnight long.
  • Hardcoded dates. Some 2026 BLS and NFP dates are hardcoded and not yet checked against bls.gov.
