What a squeeze day looks like

  • The worst shorts are mostly all-day grinds that peak in the last hour. BTBT peaked at 15:51; FLNA, BLNK, IOVA and VERU all peaked after 15:20.

Part 2

Shorting bot: a close that survives a dead computer

The plan was to send the 15:55 cover to the exchange in the morning as a market-on-close order, so it fills even if the Mac dies. Alpaca makes that harder than it sounds:

  • A stop and a market-on-close cover can't both sit on the same shares. The second one is rejected for insufficient quantity. This comes from Alpaca forum posts by staff and users, so it's the first thing to confirm on paper with 1 share.
  • Closing orders are refused from 15:50 to 19:00. Paper fills them at the closing quote, not the auction price, and can fill part and let the rest expire.
  • A live account may need Alpaca's "elite" routing for these orders. Check before going live.
Mode (STAGED_MOC_COVER)What it doesTrade-off
off (default)Today's behaviourThe close needs the computer at 15:55
handoff (recommended with the stop)Stop until 14:00, then cancel it and stage the whole short as a closing orderNo stop for the last two hours. Needs the computer once between 14:00 and 15:49. Would have saved Oct 22.
splitHalf the shares carry the stop, half the closing orderHalf the position still depends on the 15:55 run
fullA closing order for every share from 09:31, no stopFully independent of the computer. It pairs naturally with the no-stop v2.1 in Part 1.

Bug fixes (on when merged)

  • Entries waited only 30 s for fills before placing stops. Paper took 1–3 minutes at the open, so most shorts would have had no stop at all. Entries now wait up to 150 s, and a stop is never sent against an entry that's still working.
  • Stop repair: the 10:00, 12:00 and 14:00 runs now give a stop to any of today's shorts that's missing one.
  • One Alpaca error at 15:55 failed the whole close job after it had marked itself started, so the 15:50 fallback stood aside and nothing covered. The cover now retries every 10 s until the cutoff.

Also built, all off: a cover loop that never cancels a working order (today's false alarm), marketable-limit collars for entries and covers, and auto-clearing of one-off alerts. 266 tests on its branch.

Part 3

Options bot: the short sample flattered it

The agent rebuilt option prices back to 2016 for SPY and 1990 for SPX, using a volatility surface fitted to 3.5 million real Alpaca prints. Checked against real chains, it matched all 55 real dip trades, with trade P&L correlation of 0.79–0.94. Checked against Cboe's PUT and PPUT indexes from 2007, monthly correlation was 0.98–0.99. If anything it runs slightly pessimistic on the dip.

  • Dip spread: keep the signal, structure and exits as they are. No variant survives deflation. Extra names don't help: 55–80% of DIA, QQQ, XLK and IJR signals land within 2 days of a SPY signal, so they add the same tail.
  • Tail hedge: not at $15k. 1% a year in SPY put spreads cut COVID from −29% to −23% and did nothing for Feb 2018 or Aug 2024. A one-lot standing hedge costs 4–6% a year. VIX calls helped most, but Alpaca can't trade them.
  • The shorting bot isn't part of the gap problem. It held no positions on the key crash days, and its correlation with SPY on big down days is −0.31. Its risk is single-name squeezes.
Part 4

Options bot: why today's condor didn't fill

At 15:55 today the bot tried 1× SPY Dec-18 650/675 · 814/835. Mid was 2.29 and natural 2.13. It stepped from 2.28 down to 2.20 and cancelled at 15:57. Alpaca paper only fills a combo that is marketable against the national best bid and offer, with no price improvement, and the ladder stops halfway to natural, so it can only fill if the market moves. The backtest assumed about $2.09, so filling at natural is still better than the research assumed.

  • Fix (off): CONDOR_ENTRY_LADDER = "natural", and the same for dips. It walks from mid to natural and never accepts less than the backtest credit. CONDOR_ENTRY_EARLY adds a first attempt from 15:46. The options research suggests not loosening the condor at all and turning it off instead. The ladder fix still matters for the dip spreads.
  • Ex-dividend guard (on when merged): the condor's Dec 18 expiry is SPY's ex-dividend date. An in-the-money short call can be exercised early the day before, which is exactly the bot's planned close day. The guard closes the condor on the last session before an ex-date when the call is in the money and its time value is below the dividend.
  • A dead computer can't leave half a spread: Alpaca fills multi-leg orders all together or not at all. What a crash used to lose was the bookkeeping. A write-ahead journal now records each order before it's sent, and the next run books what filled.
  • Also built:
    • Options alerts to your phone.
    • Slippage against mid and natural on every order.
    • A fix for the 16:15 sync overwriting a 15:55 error with "ok".
    • A fix for the final-sweep log level that made today's clean cancel look like a failed run.
    • Resting take-profit and expiry orders (off until tested on paper).

156 tests on its branch. The sizing check confirms it: 1 condor = $2,283 max loss = 15.2% of $15k, and Alpaca's margin requirement is $2,500.

Read before acting

Caveats

  • Multiple testing. Under the strictest deflation, nothing this round, and nothing from earlier rounds, is statistically proven. The recommendations lean on how robust a result is, on the literature, and on stress. They don't lean on headline Sharpe.
  • Alpaca behaviour is partly undocumented: closing-order quantity reservation, opening-auction short acceptance, resting GTC option orders and half-day cutoffs. Every flag that depends on these stays off until a 1-share paper test passes.
  • No historical borrow data. Squeeze names were probably hard to borrow, and that isn't modelled.
  • Paper fills aren't live fills. Paper fills closing orders at the closing quote and combos only at natural. Live behaviour will differ in both directions.
